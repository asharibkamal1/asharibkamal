AI tools are changing how software gets built. But the list of tools is endless, and most "top 50 AI tools" posts are written by people who never used them. Here are the ones I actually use.

## AI for writing code

- AI coding assistants in the IDE: what they're great at (boilerplate, tests, refactors)
- Where they fail: architecture decisions, business rules, security
- My rule: never merge code you don't understand

## AI for learning and debugging

- Explaining unfamiliar code and error messages
- Generating examples for new libraries

## AI for writing and content

- Outlining blog posts and documentation
- Why I still write the final draft myself

## AI for business and productivity

- Emails, proposals, meeting notes
- Research and summarising

## How to use AI without lowering quality

1. Give context: paste the relevant code and constraints
2. Ask for reasoning, not just answers
3. Review everything; write tests
4. Keep secrets and company code out of public tools

<Callout type="warning" title="Company code">
Follow your employer's policy on AI tools. Never paste confidential code or data into tools that aren't approved.
</Callout>
